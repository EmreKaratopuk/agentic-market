# Dispute Resolution Policy

## When to Open a Dispute

A dispute should be opened when a buyer and seller cannot reach a resolution directly. Common reasons include:

- Item not received despite tracking showing delivery
- Item significantly different from the listing description
- Refund agreed by the seller but not processed within 5 business days
- Seller unresponsive for more than 3 business days
- Damaged item where the seller disputes responsibility

Buyers should first attempt to resolve the issue with the seller via in-platform messaging before opening a dispute. Disputes opened without a prior contact attempt may be delayed.

## How to Open a Dispute

1. Go to **My Orders** and select the relevant order
2. Click **Open Dispute** and select the dispute category
3. Describe the issue in the text field (be specific — include dates, amounts, and what resolution you are seeking)
4. Attach supporting evidence: photos, screenshots of seller messages, tracking records
5. Submit — both the buyer and seller are notified immediately

Disputes can only be opened within **60 days** of the order delivery date or the estimated delivery date if the item was never received.

## Evidence Requirements

Strong evidence significantly speeds up resolution. Recommended evidence by dispute type:

| Dispute Type | Evidence to Provide |
|---|---|
| Item not received | Screenshot of tracking page showing no delivery or wrong delivery |
| Item not as described | Clear photos comparing the received item to the listing |
| Defective item | Photos or short video demonstrating the defect |
| Refund not received | Screenshot of seller message confirming refund commitment |
| Wrong item sent | Photos of the item received alongside the original order confirmation |

Submitting clear evidence at the time of opening the dispute reduces resolution time significantly.

## Escalation Path

### Stage 1 — Direct Resolution (Days 1–3)

After a dispute is opened, the seller has 3 business days to respond and propose a resolution. During this period:

- The buyer and seller can exchange messages within the dispute thread
- The marketplace does not intervene unless requested
- Either party can accept a proposed resolution to close the dispute early

### Stage 2 — Marketplace Mediation (Days 4–10)

If no resolution is reached by Day 3, the marketplace assigns a case handler. The handler will:

- Review all submitted evidence from both parties
- Contact both parties for additional information if needed
- Issue a preliminary decision within 7 business days of assignment

### Stage 3 — Final Decision (Days 11–14)

If either party disputes the preliminary decision, they may submit a final appeal with new evidence within 2 business days. The marketplace issues a binding final decision within 14 business days of the original dispute opening date.

## Resolution Outcomes

Possible outcomes of a dispute:

- **Full refund to buyer**: Item price and original shipping refunded; seller bears return shipping if applicable
- **Partial refund to buyer**: Agreed amount refunded; item is kept by the buyer
- **Replacement sent**: Seller sends a replacement item at no additional cost
- **No action**: Dispute dismissed if evidence supports the seller's position or the claim is outside policy scope

## Timeframes Summary

| Stage | Duration |
|---|---|
| Seller response window | 3 business days |
| Marketplace mediation | Up to 7 business days |
| Final appeal window | 2 business days after preliminary decision |
| Maximum total resolution time | 14 business days |

## Seller Impact

Disputes resolved in the buyer's favour are recorded against the seller's account. Sellers with a dispute resolution rate above 3% of total orders in a 90-day period are subject to performance review and potential account restrictions.
